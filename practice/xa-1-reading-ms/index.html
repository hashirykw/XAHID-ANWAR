Question 2 [9]

Up to 3 marks per phrase: 1 for meaning, up to 2 for effect.

  1. Meaning: the shutter made a loud, harsh, screeching sound. Effect: the noise feels heavy and dramatic, suggesting the shop's age and the start of a working day.
  2. Meaning: the grease was very thick and slow-moving. Effect: humorous personification; it makes something ordinary feel tired and stubborn, matching the narrator's boredom.
  3. Meaning: the wheel was badly bent and twisted. Effect: the everyday image is vivid and slightly comic, but it also shows how serious the damage was and why the boy was frightened.

Question 3(a) Summary [15]

10 marks for content (1 per point, up to 10). 5 marks for writing: own words, organisation, accuracy and keeping within 150 words.

Causes

  1. Rubbish is washed down storm drains / nullahs during the monsoon.
  2. Waste comes from markets and streets far inland.
  3. Single-use plastic is the biggest problem.
  4. It is cheap and light, so it is easily blown away.
  5. Very little is collected for recycling.
  6. Visitors leave rubbish behind on busy days.
  7. There are too few bins.
  8. Bins are emptied irregularly.

Actions

  1. Volunteer clean-ups collect large amounts of waste.
  2. Schools run awareness weeks.
  3. Some shopkeepers charge for plastic bags, reducing their use.
  4. Researchers suggest nets at storm-drain mouths to catch rubbish before it reaches the sea.
